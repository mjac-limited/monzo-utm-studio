What are UTM tags?

UTM stands for Urchin Tracking Module, a convention introduced by Urchin Software (later acquired by Google) for attaching identity to a URL. In practice, UTMs are five short query-string parameters appended to any link that points to your website. When someone clicks a tagged link, the browser carries those parameters along, and Google Analytics 4 reads them and stores exactly where the visitor came from, what channel they were using, and which campaign or piece of content drove them.

Without them, the vast majority of inbound traffic ends up labelled as direct or (not set) in your reports - a black hole that makes it impossible to know which marketing efforts are working.

Why they matter

The simplest way to think about UTMs is as a labelling system for your traffic. Every time you put a link to your site somewhere - a partner article, a sales email, a tweet, a QR code on a conference banner - you have a one-time opportunity to tell your analytics where that link is coming from. Skip the tag, and that opportunity is gone. The visitor still arrives, but with no context.

Across a year of campaigns this matters enormously. Untagged traffic is the single largest cause of attribution gaps inside GA4, and it's almost entirely fixable with discipline at the link-building stage. The tool you're using right now exists for exactly that reason: to make tagging fast, consistent, and idiot-proof.

Anatomy of a tagged URL

A UTM-tagged URL is just your normal destination URL with a query string of utm_* key-value pairs glued to the end. Here's a tagged link broken down:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=producthunt.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=launch_week&utm_content=hero_banner

The bit before the ? is your destination, where the visitor actually goes. The bit after is the metadata GA4 picks up. The order of the parameters doesn't matter; what matters is that the names match exactly (utm_source, not UTM_Source or utmSource) and that the values follow a consistent convention.

The five parameters

There are exactly five UTM parameters. Four of them are commonly required for any tagged link; the fifth is optional and contextual.

utm_source

Identifies where the traffic came from - the referring website, platform, or tool. For partner placements this is usually the partner's domain. For social posts it's the platform name. For email it's the sending platform.

producthunt.com linkedin customer.io saastr_2026
utm_medium

The channel type. GA4's default channel grouping reads this directly, so values must follow a strict taxonomy or your reports break. Stick to: referral, email, outreach, event, social, cpc. Inventing your own values (e.g. e-mail or partnership) will land your traffic in (other) in GA4.

referral email outreach event social cpc
utm_campaign

Groups all links that belong to one marketing initiative. This is the dimension you'll filter by when measuring a campaign's performance. Use lowercase with underscores; never use spaces. The same campaign name should appear on every tagged link for that initiative - across email, social, paid, etc. - so you can compare channel performance for a single campaign.

refer_a_friend spring_promo monthly_digest event_sponsorship
utm_content

Distinguishes multiple links within the same campaign. The classic case: an email with three CTAs all pointing to the same landing page. Without utm_content, all three clicks look identical in GA4. With it, you can see whether the hero CTA outperformed the footer link.

hero_cta comparison_table qr_code reel
utm_term

Originally intended for paid-search keywords, but it's a general-purpose slot for any extra dimension you want to filter by. Common second uses: an audience segment in email (existing_customers), an A/B variant ID, or a prospect company name in BD outreach.

existing_customers enterprise variant_a acme_corp

When to use each channel

Each channel in this builder corresponds to a specific real-world scenario. Picking the right channel matters because the medium is locked per channel, which is what keeps your GA4 reports clean.

Outreach

You're sending a prospect or partner a link to your product directly - typically through a sales tool, BD email, or LinkedIn message. Use the tool or team as utm_source, set utm_term to the prospect's company name, and you'll be able to filter GA4 by deal or account later.

Referral

A partner or review site is adding a link to your product on their domain. Use the partner domain as utm_source. The medium stays locked to referral so the traffic falls into the right GA4 default-channel bucket.

Email

You're sending a campaign or transactional email. Set utm_source to the sending platform (Customer.io, Braze, Mailchimp). Use utm_content to tell apart different CTAs in the same email - for instance, hero_cta versus footer_link.

Event

You're at a conference with a QR code on a banner, a slide in a talk, or a brochure on a stand. Use the event name as utm_source and put the physical format in utm_content - qr_code, brochure, speaker_slide.

Paid (cpc)

You're running paid search or display advertising. Set utm_medium to cpc so GA4 maps it to the Paid Search channel. Use utm_term for the keyword and utm_content to differentiate ad variants when running tests.

Filtering reports in GA4

Once your links are tagged consistently, the payoff is in GA4. Head to Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition in the GA4 sidebar. The default report breaks traffic down by channel grouping (the utm_medium bucket). Add a secondary dimension of Session campaign to drill into specific campaigns; switch to Session source to see which partners or platforms are driving the most visits.

For deeper analysis, build a custom exploration with Session campaign, Session source / medium, and Session manual ad content as dimensions. That gives you a full breakdown of every tagged link by campaign, channel, and creative variant.

If your reports show a lot of (not set) values, it usually means links somewhere are missing tags. Treat that number as your hygiene KPI - it should be small.

Naming hygiene & best practices

Three rules will save you from 90% of attribution headaches.

  • Lowercase, always. GA4 treats Email and email as different values. Pick lowercase and never deviate.
  • Underscores, never spaces. Spaces break URLs and create ugly %20 sequences in your reports.
  • One campaign name per initiative, repeated everywhere. The whole point of utm_campaign is to roll multiple channels up into one number. If you tag email with spring_promo and paid with spring-promo, you'll see two campaigns in GA4 instead of one.

Tag every outbound link that lands on your site, every time. Untagged links quietly pollute analytics and the cleanup work later is far more painful than the 30 seconds it takes to use this builder.
